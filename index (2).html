Spacecraft Fire Safety Data

Decades of microgravity combustion experiments, summarized and compared in one place. Find the investigations that matter for prevention, detection, and suppression in spacecraft, space stations, lunar and Martian bases, and pressurized rovers.

Why fire in space is different

Without buoyancy, a candle flame becomes a ghostly blue sphere that depends on diffusion for its oxygen. Flammability also climbs with oxygen concentration, which matters because planned Moon bases and rovers are expected to run oxygen-richer than Earth air.

Test conditions: oxygen and pressure

Flammability depends strongly on oxygen concentration, and in microgravity on airflow as well. Bars show oxygen concentration for the environments in the challenge brief.

Spacesuits

100% oxygen at only 4.3 psi (Earth sea level is 14.7 psi). Astronauts pre-breathe 100% oxygen to expel nitrogen and avoid decompression sickness.

Moon base and rovers

An intermediate atmosphere is expected, likely 30% oxygen or more at 10.2 psi or less, to shorten pre-breathing. That is over 40% more oxygen than Earth air, making materials much more flammable.

Highest-oxygen tests

CFI-G (ACME) flowed up to 85% oxygen from a spherical burner into a nitrogen and flammable-gas chamber, creating an inside-out flame.

Variables to compare: oxygen concentration, airflow speed, fuel shape and size, and sensors or measurements. Check how tests within one investigation differed and what stayed the same.

Fire safety areas

NASA's spacecraft fire safety work is organized around prevention, detection, and suppression. Select an area to see its related investigations.

Prevention

Control ignition sources and choose materials using flammability limits. Studies focus on solids such as fabrics and plastics.

BASS, BASS-II, Saffire, BRE

Detection

Smoke and aerosol measurement in microgravity to inform fire detectors.

SAME, SAME-R, DAFT

Suppression

FLEX diluted the chamber atmosphere with carbon dioxide and helium, both of which suppress fire.

FLEX, FLEX-2
Earth applications: Fine-water-mist extinguishers developed for the ISS have terrestrial uses, and NASA technology has improved smoke detection and firefighter gear such as protective clothing and air tanks. Rocket and propellant fires are outside the scope of PSI data.
