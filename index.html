Fire Safety Explorer
Challenge brief

Spacecraft Fire Safety Data

Every combustion experiment table in NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics database, cleaned into one comparable dataset, ranked against the atmosphere of the vehicle or habitat you are designing, and annotated with what each result means for prevention, detection and suppression.

What the data says

Computed directly from the PSI experiment tables. Every number on this site traces back to a PSI investigation and its DOI.

Partial gravity: PSI has no lunar- or Martian-gravity combustion data yet. Microgravity and 1-g results bound the behaviour in between, and ACME E-FIELD Flames is the only partial-gravity analog (electric field acting on flame ions). LUCI and FM2 are expected to fill this gap.

Each dot is one test (or one condition row for summary tables). Shaded boxes are crew-environment targets. Hollow dots use assumed cabin-air values. Hover for details; click legend items to hide fuel types.

Oxygen at a glance

Why these targets: spacesuits use 100% O₂ at 4.3 psi, so astronauts pre-breathe oxygen to avoid decompression sickness. To shorten pre-breathe, Moon bases and rovers are expected to use an intermediate atmosphere of about 30% O₂ or more at 10.2 psi or less: over 40% more oxygen than Earth air, making materials much more flammable. The 85% points are ACME inverse flames, where oxygen flows out of a spherical burner into a nitrogen-and-fuel chamber.

Data for predictive modeling

Flammability and fire-scenario models need two things from experiments: the conditions that were set (inputs) and the results that were measured (outputs). We extracted both from every PSI experiment table into one file with consistent units.

Model-ready datasets

Investigations that vary at least one condition and report at least one per-test measured result for 20+ tests: the datasets you can fit or validate a model against directly. (Daily "flames ignited" tallies do not count.)

Explore relationships

What each investigation provides

Data dictionary

Before you fit a model: adiabatic flame temperature and stoichiometric mixture fraction are calculated by the investigators, not measured. FLEX-2 rows are condition ranges, not single tests. ACME inverse-flame O₂ is the burner stream. Flame images, soot fields, radiometer and thermocouple time series stay in the raw PSI data files; use the PSI record links to download them. Fire safety pillars

NASA's spacecraft fire safety work is organized around prevention, detection and suppression. Listed investigations are rated 2 or 3 (out of 3) for that pillar.

Related studies without experiment tables

These PSI studies have no test table, so they are not ranked or charted. The ground studies are PSI-funded models built on the flight data above, evidence of which datasets already support predictive modeling.

Earth applications: SAME results feed smoke-detector design, FLEX informs gaseous suppressant effectiveness, and the high-oxygen ACME data applies to oxygen-enriched settings such as hospitals. See Earth Applications. Rocket and propellant fires are outside the scope of PSI data.

Earth applications

Fire physics that is hidden by gravity on Earth becomes measurable in microgravity, and the results carry back to fire safety on the ground. Each card names the PSI investigations behind it, and the tag says how strong the link is.

Same problem, two places: a Moon base at 30%+ O₂ and a hospital room with supplemental oxygen face the same hazard: materials ignite more easily and burn faster in oxygen-enriched air. The high-oxygen ACME and FLEX data in PSI informs both.

How to read the tags

  • PSI dataPSI experiment data directly measures the physics behind this application (listed investigations).
  • RelatedPSI data informs the models or understanding, but the application also depends on other testing.
Not covered by PSI data: the challenge brief also cites NASA spinoffs such as fine-water-mist extinguishers and firefighter protective clothing and air tanks. These came from other NASA programs; PSI contains no test data on them, so we list only applications the PSI combustion data actually supports.

How the relevance score works

score = 0.40·conditions + 0.25·pillar_focus + 0.20·fuel_realism + 0.15·data_amount

closeness(test) = exp(−[(ΔO2/8 pts)² + (ΔP/0.25 atm)²]) (× 0.7 if the value is assumed)
conditions = ½·best closeness + ½·min(1, 3 × share of tests with closeness > 0.5)
pillar_focus = weighted mean of prevention/detection/suppression ratings (0–3); default weights depend on the environment and are adjustable
fuel_realism: solid 1.0 · solid emulator 0.9 · smoke source 0.8 · liquid 0.6 · gas 0.5 · aerosol 0.3–0.4
data_amount = log(tests+1) / log(300), capped at 1
  • DistancesΔO2 and ΔP are the gap between a test's range and the target range (0 if they overlap).
  • Assumed valuesWhere a table does not record O2 or pressure and the investigation ran in ISS cabin air, we use 21% and 1 atm, flag the row, and down-weight it.
  • Invalid valuesPhysically impossible entries (e.g. 0 atm, 200% O2, negative flow) are blanked, never guessed, and noted in the row.
  • Inverse flamesIn ACME inverse flames the O2 value is the oxidizer stream from the burner, not the ambient atmosphere. Use the filter on the condition map.
  • InterpretationInsight cards were drafted with AI assistance from the PSI investigation records and reviewed by the team. Confirm critical details against the PSI pages and publications.
